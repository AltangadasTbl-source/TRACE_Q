**Classification ambiguity:** None. The explicitly different analyses are an interpretive alternative, not a document-role ambiguity.

**Source evidence:** Absolute rate difference 3.38 per 100 patient-weeks, 95% CrI −0.57 to 7.94; adjusted RR 1.72, 95% CI 1.03 to 2.87; adjusted P=.04. General rule is two-sided P<.05.


**Reported-versus-comparator:** Absolute ED difference 3.38 (95% CrI −0.57 to 7.94) gives a null-containing absolute interval. Separately adjusted RR 1.72 (95% CI 1.03 to 2.87), P=.04, gives a null-excluding relative interval and significant adjusted test. The RR interval and its own P agree.

**Reasoning procedure:** Compare each interval to its own null and retain the disclosed analysis distinction. Zero lies in the absolute-effect CrI; the RR CI excludes one and agrees with its adjusted P.

**Calculation:** −0.57<0<7.94 and .04<.05. Endpoint half-unit intervals [−.575,−.565] and [7.935,7.945], P interval [.035,.045]; null-value readings persist. Relative discrepancy is not applicable to a category-1 logical decision conflict.

**Affected result or extracted value:** ED absolute rate difference/CrI versus adjusted RR/CI/P and their inferential readings.

**Why context does not resolve it:** Route A category 1 requires registration even when interval and P are explicitly attributed to different analyses.

**Alternative source-grounded interpretations:** Footnote b uses a treatment-plus-random-site model for marginalized absolute effects, while the RR additionally adjusts the two stratification factors. Bayesian credible and frequentist confidence intervals have different interpretations. Do not claim that P=.04 tests the absolute difference or that the reported RR test fails to reconcile.

**Mechanical evidence recheck:** Every cited source location was found and the source/comparator values matched. Direct Table 3 page-image confirmation established column alignment and the different covariate sets. The null comparisons and half-unit intervals reproduced without assuming a common model.
