### F-XS-004

- **Locations and roles:** PLAN [protocol page 21, section 13.3](../../../joi250034supp1_prod_1750956984.09018.pdf#page=21); PLAN [SAP page 10, interim timing](../../../joi250034supp2_prod_1750956984.11521.pdf#page=10) and [SAP page 11, stopping thresholds](../../../joi250034supp2_prod_1750956984.11521.pdf#page=11); REPORT [main page 3, interim paragraph](../../../jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf#page=3).
- **Printed specification:** the protocol and SAP specify review after 50% recruitment, a primary-composite net-benefit/harm formal threshold P<.001, and separately a mortality stopping threshold P<.0027. The main report says the committee conducted one interim analysis after 50% recruitment and that a recommendation to stop early “required a difference in perinatal mortality” indicated by a 3-SD Haybittle-Peto boundary (P<.003).
- **Comparison logic:** timing matches, and .0027 versus .003 is compatible with displayed precision. The unresolved comparison is the necessary event criterion: the plan permits advice based on convincing benefit/harm in the primary composite independently of its separate mortality rule, whereas the report says an early-stop recommendation required a mortality difference. These are different decision rules, not differently rounded thresholds.
- **Focus area:** D. **Proposed review domain:** protocol_report. **Proposed relationship:** P_R.
- **Qualifying route:** Route B.
- **Affected result or extracted value:** the interim decision rule governing continuation and the stated circumstances in which the primary-composite trial could stop.
- **Why context does not resolve it:** “required” makes the reported mortality condition necessary; the plan's distinct primary-composite condition could trigger advice without mortality satisfying its threshold. The report does not say the composite condition was retained or withdrawn.
- **Supported alternatives:** the report may be a shortened description of only the mortality rule; the committee charter may have changed or clarified the protocol rules; or the composite criterion may actually have been dropped. No committee charter or amendment resolving this is supplied.
- **Exact human question:** At the interim analysis, could the committee recommend early stopping under the prespecified primary-composite P<.001 rule without a mortality difference, or was mortality the necessary stopping condition stated in the report?

### F-XS-005

- **Locations and roles:** PLAN [protocol page 17, section 8](../../../joi250034supp1_prod_1750956984.09018.pdf#page=17); PLAN [SAP page 14, section 3.11](../../../joi250034supp2_prod_1750956984.11521.pdf#page=14) and [SAP page 29, proposed Table 15](../../../joi250034supp2_prod_1750956984.11521.pdf#page=29); REPORT [main page 4, prespecified subgroup paragraph](../../../jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf#page=4) and [main page 8, Figure 2](../../../jama_kumar_2025_oi_250034_1750956984.08518.pdf#page=8). SAP confirms the reported criteria; the direct conflict is protocol versus report.
- **Printed criteria and values:** protocol subgroup 4 is “one or more previous CS,” while main page 4 specifies “cesarean delivery in preceding pregnancy,” matching SAP Table 15. Figure 2's prior-CS yes subgroup gives 4/57 versus 1/71, RR 4.98 (0.57–43.35), and its no subgroup 79/1568 versus 83/1554, RR 0.94 (0.70–1.27). Protocol subgroup 6 is “major fetal anomaly in this pregnancy,” while main page 4 and the SAP specify “post-natal diagnosis of fetal anomaly,” without the major-anomaly criterion. Figure 2's anomaly yes subgroup gives 7/19 versus 4/22, RR 2.03 (0.70–5.87); no gives 76/1606 versus 80/1603, RR 0.95 (0.70–1.29).
- **Comparison logic:** the six-item lists were compared item by item. Small-for-gestational-age <10th centile, multiple pregnancy, pre-eclampsia and noncephalic presentation agree. The two remaining items specify different membership criteria: a woman with an earlier CS followed by a vaginal birth meets the protocol's any-prior-CS criterion but not the preceding-pregnancy-CS criterion; dropping “major” from the anomaly criterion can change included cases, while the postnatal ascertainment criterion is explicit in the later specification. No actual unreported case reclassification is inferred. The observable issue is the changed specification attached to reported primary-effect subgroup results. This is a categorical comparison; numeric display-precision discrepancy is not applicable.
- **Focus area:** D. **Proposed review domain:** protocol_report. **Proposed relationship:** P_R.
- **Qualifying route:** Route B.
- **Affected result or extracted value:** the population criteria attached to the prior-caesarean and fetal-anomaly subgroup RRs and their interaction analyses.
- **Why context does not resolve it:** the criteria are not interchangeable abbreviations: any prior CS differs from CS in the last pregnancy, and a major-anomaly restriction differs from an unrestricted anomaly description. The report states the analyses were prespecified, but does not explain the revision from the protocol criteria to the SAP criteria or establish identical membership.
- **Supported alternatives:** the SAP may constitute a prospective amendment superseding these protocol subgroup criteria; the collected categories may happen to yield identical membership; or the report labels may shorten more restrictive implemented definitions. The package does not establish which applies.
- **Exact human question:** Which membership criteria were actually used for the prior-caesarean and fetal-anomaly subgroups, and was the change from the protocol criteria prospectively approved or did both definitions yield the same analyzed participants?
