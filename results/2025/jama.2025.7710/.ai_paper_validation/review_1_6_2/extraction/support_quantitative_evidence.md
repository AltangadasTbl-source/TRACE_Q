Background

In Australia, almost 1 in 4 caesarean sections are performed for suspected
fetal distress. A fetus can become distressed when the placenta is unable to
adequately provide it with sufficient oxygen and nutrients during labour. The
only therapeutic options when fetal distress develops are emergency operative
birth by caesarean section (CS) or instrumental vaginal birth (IVB) – both of
which can have serious consequences. Sildenafil Citrate (SC) preferentially
dilates pelvic blood vessels and increases utero-placental blood flow. We
propose that oral SC improves uterine perfusion in labour thus reducing
adverse perinatal outcomes due to fetal distress.

Aim

1 BACKGROUND
Hypoxia during labour causes fetal distress, a key factor in 23% of emergency caesarean births in
Australia.3 Hypoxic injury can lead to intrapartum stillbirth, severe neonatal morbidity, neonatal
encephalopathy (NE) and cerebral palsy. 4 5 6 NE is a major risk factor for cerebral palsy. Eighty
percent of infants with moderate or severe NE suffer hypoxic brain injury near the time of birth - in
>50% of cases this injury occurs during labour.7 Other than emergency operative birth by Caesarean
Section (CS) or by Instrumental Vaginal Birth (IVB), we lack therapeutic options to achieve safe
outcomes for the infant.
Sildenafil Citrate preferentially dilates pelvic vessels, increasing utero-placental perfusion.8 It was
originally used in hypertension and ischaemic heart disease and is effective in adult and newborn
pulmonary hypertension.9 10 In our systematic review of 16 maternal studies, side effects of SC were
mild with no long-term adverse outcomes.11
Pilot data from the RidStress Phase 2 RCT
We showed in a world-first Phase 2 RCT (RidStress) of 300 women at term, in a Report of Major
Impact in the American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynaecology in January 2020,12 that up to 3 oral
doses of SC vs placebo: (a) halved CS or IVB for fetal distress [RR 0.49, 95% Confidence Interval
(CI) 0.33 - 0.73), p=0.0004] (b) halved the duration of the 2nd stage of labour (36 vs. 66 min, p=0.02)
and (c) halved fetal heart rate (FHR) abnormalities [15% vs. 32%; RR 0.48, 95% CI 0.31 - 0.75,
p=0.0009]. The trial however lacked power to evaluate neonatal outcomes.
In partnership with the Mater Research Institute-University of Queensland, NHMRC Clinical Trials
Centre (CTC), Stillbirth Centre for Research Excellence and the Australian Cerebral Palsy Alliance,
this MRFF funded, 4-year, Phase 3 RCT will test if SC reduces the relative risk of a primary
composite endpoint of ten perinatal outcomes attributable to intrapartum hypoxia by 35%, from 7%
to 4.55%, in 3200 women in spontaneous or induced labour at term in 16 Australian hospitals.
iSEARCH is the world’s first Phase 3 trial to re-purpose SC - a widely available, well characterised,
affordable, off-patent vasodilator drug - by evaluating whether, compared to Placebo, it improves
perinatal and maternal outcomes by reducing operative birth for fetal distress. These are major areas
of unmet need, identified as critical priorities by The Lancet.13 14 iSEARCH will also contribute to a
separately funded, international Individual Participant Data Prospective Meta-Analysis (IPD PMA)
powered to show a 10% to 20% reduction in intrapartum stillbirth, 28 day neonatal mortality and
other severe neonatal morbidity attributable to intrapartum hypoxia. The IPD PMA will include
multiple concurrent international trials in high, low- and middle income countries following a shared
protocol, funded by relevant national and international bodies and guided by an international Data
and Safety Monitoring Committee with an agreed Charter.12
The STRIDER Trials: The international STRIDER (Sildenafil Therapy In Dismal prognosis Earlyonset severe intrauterine growth Restriction) RCTs used daily SC doses of 75 mg SC for up to 10
weeks (~5,250 mg in total).15,16 In 2019, The Dutch STRIDER trial was stopped due to a nonsignificant increase in neonatal deaths and an increase in persistent pulmonary hypertension of the
newborn in the SC group.17 Although a subsequent meta-analysis of data from 329 infants from the
Australia-New Zealand, United Kingdom and Netherlands trials showed no difference in the risk of
neonatal death or persistent pulmonary hypertension,18 the investigators recommend that SC is not
prescribed outside of clinical trials.19 The indication and dosage of SC in the iSEARCH trial are
however very different from the STRIDER trials as we will only use up to 150mg in women >37 weeks
gestation.

If the study hypotheses are confirmed, wider implementation in other healthcare settings, particularly
in low- and middle-income countries (LMIC) would also be likely, thus benefiting regions with the

Rationale for Sildenafil Citrate to reduce intrapartum hypoxia and fetal distress
Most cases of fetal distress in labour reflect some degree of pre-existing placental impairment24
which reduces the capacity of the fetus to cope with the stress of uterine contractions. 25 In many
term pregnancies, uterine blood flow can fall by 60% during contractions26 provoking unexpected
fetal distress. Fetal decompensation occurs27 if there is insufficient time for placental reperfusion
between contractions.24 Fetal distress is a major factor in 1 in 4 emergency CS in Australia3 and is
associated with significant perinatal morbidity and mortality (hypoxic ischaemic encephalopathy,
cerebral palsy, intrapartum stillbirth and neonatal death).4 5 6 The only therapeutic options in
response to fetal distress are emergency operative birth by CS or IVB – both of which have serious
consequences including complications in future pregnancies.28,29 There is an urgent unmet need for
effective interventions to reduce perinatal mortality due to intrapartum stillbirth 30 and improve
maternal and neonatal outcomes by reducing fetal distress and emergency operative births in labour.
We31 have shown that a low fetal cerebro-placental ratio (CPR) on ultrasound identifies babies at
increased risk of intrapartum fetal distress. We also showed that women with low pre-labour levels
of placental growth factor (PlGF) are at similar risk.32 However, neither the CPR, PlGF nor
computerised analysis of fetal heart rate patterns33 or fetal ECG,34 have been shown to improve
important perinatal outcomes, further illustrating why this is a critical area of unmet need.

13.1 Sample Size
The incidence of the composite adverse outcome is estimated at 7% based on data in a recent large
RCT in term pregnancies54 and the Australian and New Zealand Neonatal Network. To detect a 35%
reduction from 7% to 4.55% for an alpha of 0.05 and >80% power with a 10% drop-out in each arm,
needs 3,200 women, also yielding >90% power to detect a 25% reduction for the secondary outcome
(CS or IVB for fetal distress).

generalised linear models (binary or normal). Intervention effect will be presented as relative risk
(RR) or mean difference (MD), as appropriate, with 95% confidence intervals. Two-tailed P values

needed to treat to prevent one adverse outcome will be calculated. Where there are differences in
the baseline characteristics between the two treatment groups that might be associated with
outcomes, secondary analyses of the primary outcome will be carried out using multiple (logbinominal) regression. Reporting will follow CONSORT and TRIPOD guidelines.

composite outcome (Section 2.2.1 above). P values adjusted for the ten comparisons performed for

Analyses of the primary and secondary outcomes will adhere to the intention-to-treat principal using

primary analysis provided that the models are numerically stable and converge well. Maternal
